Make room for the web.

Block unwanted requests before they load, and hide the spaces ads leave behind.

Cookie choices, handled.

Automatically submits your preferences on recognized OneTrust, Cookiebot, Complianz, and CookieYes dialogs. Applies across websites where Safari allows Harbor access.

Unsupported or ambiguous dialogs stay visible for manual choice. This does not delete existing cookies or override a site’s previous consent. Pausing Harbor for a site also pauses cookie automation.

    Your rules, your web.

    Add URL patterns or hide elements with CSS. Rules saved by the element picker appear here.

    One rule per line. For example:
    ||ads.example.com^
    news.example.com##.sponsored
    @@||cdn.example.com/needed.js

    Edit or remove a line, then save. Unsupported syntax is rejected with a line number. No scripts, redirects, or procedural filters are executed.

    These filters stay on this device. Pausing protection also pauses custom rules.

    Enable Harbor in Safari

    1. Open iPhone or iPad Settings → Apps → Safari → Extensions → Harbor. On older iOS versions, Safari appears directly in Settings.
    2. Turn Harbor on. Allow website access on the sites you want to filter, or choose All Websites → Allow for browsing-wide coverage.
    3. In Safari, open the page menu, choose Manage Extensions if needed, and enable Harbor. Open Harbor for site controls.
    4. Reload open pages. If Safari’s own “Turn Off Content Blockers” setting is active for a site, turn content blockers back on.

    Private Browsing has its own extension permission. Harbor cannot read or change Safari’s permission settings for you.

    What to expect

    Harbor blocks supported ad and tracker requests and hides matching elements. Safari limits extension APIs: desktop scriptlets, arbitrary request interception, and guaranteed YouTube or other video-ad removal are not included. It only protects Safari websites, not other apps.

    No account, analytics, or browsing-history log. Website access lets Harbor apply cosmetic filters and its element picker. Your exceptions and custom rules are stored locally in Safari extension storage. Uninstalling removes that configuration.
